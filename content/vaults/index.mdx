# How vaults work

Zipcode provides four things in one place: **the hardware**, **the financing**, **the management of that hardware**, and **a way to earn from it**.

Every loan is a **vault**: one specific hardware purchase, financed half by the borrower and half by lenders. The terms are the same for every vault:

| Term | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Borrower contribution | 50% of the purchase price, deposited up front |
| Lender contribution | The remaining 50% |
| Interest rate | 10% fixed APR |
| Loan term | 36 months, repaid in equal monthly payments |
| Collateral | The hardware itself, held by Zipcode until the loan is repaid |

There is no credit application or credit score. The borrower's deposit and Zipcode's custody of the hardware are the underwriting.

<Callout type="info">
  Vaults and figures shown on zipcode.finance are illustrative until launch.
</Callout>

## Who is involved

| Participant | Role |
| --- | --- |
| **Borrower** | Chooses the hardware, opens the vault, funds 50%, operates the hardware over SSH, and makes the monthly payments. |
| **Lender** | Funds any share of a vault's lender side and earns 10% fixed APR from the borrower's payments. |
| **Zipcode** | Sources the hardware, takes delivery, holds it in custody, manages and maintains it, and services the loan. |
| **Colocation facility** | A verified data center that hosts the hardware with power, cooling and network connectivity. |
| **Compute networks** | Instant, Chutes, Engy, Lium and Targon: the networks where borrowers can deploy the hardware to earn. |

## The lifecycle, end to end

<Steps>

### The borrower opens a vault

The borrower picks the hardware configuration (for example, 4× RTX PRO 6000 or 8× H200) and opens a vault for it. They deposit 50% of the purchase price. See [For Borrowers](/vaults/borrowers).

### Lenders fill the other half

The vault's lender side is open to anyone, in any size, until it is fully funded. Every lender earns the same 10% fixed APR. See [For Lenders](/vaults/lenders).

### Zipcode procures the hardware

Once the vault is fully funded, Zipcode purchases the hardware and takes delivery.

### The hardware goes into a verified colocation facility

Zipcode installs the hardware in a verified colocation facility and remains responsible for managing and maintaining it for the life of the loan. See [Hardware & Operations](/vaults/operations).

### The borrower gets access and puts it to work

The borrower receives secure SSH access to their hardware and can deploy it on supported compute networks (Instant, Chutes, Engy, Lium and Targon) to earn. See [Earning on Networks](/vaults/earning).

### The borrower repays monthly

The borrower makes 36 equal monthly payments, which are paid out to the vault's lenders.

### Title transfers

When the final payment clears, title to the hardware transfers to the borrower. If payments are missed, the hardware is sold to make lenders whole. See [Repayment, Title & Default](/vaults/repayment).

</Steps>

## A worked example

The illustrative 4× RTX PRO 6000 vault, priced at $36,000:

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Purchase price | $36,000 |
| Borrower deposit (50%) | $18,000 |
| Financed by lenders (50%) | $18,000 |
| Monthly payment (10% APR, 36 months) | $581 |
| Total repaid over the term | $20,909 |
| Total interest paid to lenders | $2,909 |

The same terms apply at every size. The illustrative 8× H200 vault ($280,000) carries a monthly payment of about $4,517, and the 2× HGX B300 vault ($900,000) about $14,520.
